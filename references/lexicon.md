# The Archetypal Lexicon

This is a tuning fork, not an encyclopedia. Each entry fixes the *canonical core*
of a symbol so interpretations stay anchored across readings. You already know
these symbols fluently — the entry exists to prevent drift, not to teach you.
Compose every combination fresh from these cores: the planet is the noun (who
acts), the sign the adverb (how), the house the setting (where), the aspect the
verb (how the characters relate).

One rule above all: a placement is never a verdict. It is a character with a
drive, a shadow, and a developmental arc — and the arc is the point.

---

## Planets — the nouns (who acts)

**Sun** — The drive to *become* someone: the hero's journey toward a coherent
self. Not who you are but who you are learning to be; a life task, not a given.
Shadow: performing identity instead of forging it. Arc: from seeking reflection
to generating light.

**Moon** — The needing, remembering animal: what safety felt like before words,
and what the body still reaches for under stress. The inner child and the inner
mother at once. Shadow: mistaking the familiar for the necessary. Arc: from
being had by needs to knowing and tending them.

**Mercury** — The messenger between worlds: how the mind perceives, names,
connects, and trades. Shadow: cleverness as armor; narrating life instead of
living it. Arc: from collecting information to making meaning.

**Venus** — The valuer: what is beautiful, what is worth wanting, how one
attracts and is attracted. The Eros principle — relatedness through pleasure and
worth. Shadow: pleasing as a substitute for valuing oneself. Arc: from being
chosen to choosing.

**Mars** — The will in the body: desire, anger, courage, the capacity to say no
and to go get. Shadow: aggression turned sideways (passive, displaced) or turned
inward. Arc: from reactive fire to directed force.

**Jupiter** — The meaning-maker: the urge toward the larger picture — growth,
faith, generosity, the intuition that life is going somewhere. Shadow:
inflation; promising more than any life can deliver; the grass elsewhere. Arc:
from appetite for more to trust in enough.

**Saturn** — The reality principle and the inner elder: limitation, time,
mastery, the fear that guards the exact spot where authority must be earned.
Where Saturn sits, the person feels behind — and eventually, if the work is
done, becomes the expert. Shadow: the inner critic mistaken for the truth. Arc:
from dread of the task to authorship of it.

**Uranus** — The awakener: the intolerance of the false, the lightning that
breaks what has calcified. Individuality as a force of nature. Shadow: rebellion
as reflex; freedom that never lands anywhere. Arc: from shock and rupture to
authentic difference, lived calmly.

**Neptune** — The longing for the ocean: dissolution of boundaries, imagination,
compassion, the ache for something this world doesn't quite provide. Shadow:
escape, fog, martyrdom, enchantment that will not survive daylight. Arc: from
longing to leave the world to bringing the water back to it.

**Pluto** — The underworld: power, death-and-rebirth, the compost heap of the
psyche where what is buried does not stay dead but transforms. Shadow: control
as a defense against powerlessness; fascination with what one fears. Arc: from
surviving to being remade — the phoenix, not the fortress.

**Chiron** — The wound that teaches: the place of injury that never fully closes
and precisely thereby becomes the source of medicine for others. Not a planet of
the ego's family — a mentor from outside it. Shadow: identifying with the wound.
Arc: from ashamed patient to wounded healer.

**North Node / South Node** — The axis of becoming. The South Node is a
competence overbuilt in the exact shape of an early lack: the end the child
reached for because it was already working, once the other end had been stepped
on. The North Node is the unbuilt capacity — **the work, not the reward.** The
destination is the life the person says they want; the North Node is the
currency that buys it, which is why pointing at it is never handing someone a
prize. Both ends are light and only one has been lived; the shadow sits at
neither pole but in the refusal to cross, and it shows up as contempt for people
who live easily at the other end. Read the axis as a loop, not two places:
gripping the South end makes the North end ache, and the ache tightens the grip.
Never read it flat — soft aspects and benefics touching the axis open it, hard
aspects clamp it, and the malefics' addresses say how hard.

## Signs — the adverbs (how the drive moves)

Every sign holds **two opposite manifestations**, one basic and one evolved, and
they are not a contradiction — they are the same principle at two altitudes.
Taurus runs from sensory indulgence to spiritual non-attachment; Scorpio from an
appetite for darkness to the offering of healing; Virgo from obsessive
self-improvement to genuinely selfless service; Gemini from moral irreverence to
spiritual innocence; Sagittarius from carefree freedom-seeking to moral
leadership; Leo from play to responsibility; Aquarius from fierce singularity to
the unity of a group; Pisces from treating life as a cosmic joke to real
surrender. Establish *which altitude is running* before deciding what is
developed and what is not. The same placement presents as either, and reading
the wrong altitude produces a portrait of a stranger.

**Aries** (cardinal fire) — Firstly, urgently, by igniting. The courage to
begin; the innocence of the warrior. Itch: patience — what happens after the
charge.

**Taurus** (fixed earth) — Slowly, sensually, by rooting. Worth measured in what
endures and can be touched. Itch: letting go — discovering that security is not
the same as stillness.

**Gemini** (mutable air) — Curiously, doubly, by connecting. Everything is
interesting and everything has a twin. Itch: depth — staying long enough for
meaning to form.

**Cancer** (cardinal water) — Protectively, tidally, by feeling first. Memory
and belonging as navigation. Itch: exposure — letting the shell open from
inside.

**Leo** (fixed fire) — Radiantly, wholeheartedly, by creating. Life as
self-expression; the heart wants witness. Itch: humility — shining without
needing the mirror.

**Virgo** (mutable earth) — Precisely, usefully, by refining. Devotion through
craft; the sacred in the detail. Itch: mercy — for the imperfect, especially
oneself.

**Libra** (cardinal air) — Harmoniously, relationally, by weighing. The self
discovered through the other; beauty as justice. Itch: decision — choosing even
when it costs the peace.

**Scorpio** (fixed water) — Intensely, totally, beneath the surface. Truth over
comfort; intimacy as ordeal and reward. Itch: surrender — power shared rather
than held.

**Sagittarius** (mutable fire) — Expansively, honestly, toward the horizon.
Meaning is out there and the arrow is already flying. Itch: presence — the
horizon is also here.

**Capricorn** (cardinal earth) — Strategically, patiently, by climbing.
Structure as devotion; the long game. Itch: tenderness — what the summit was
for.

**Aquarius** (fixed air) — Independently, systemically, from the outside
looking in. Loyalty to the future and to principle. Itch: belonging — joining
the species one observes so well.

**Pisces** (mutable water) — Porously, compassionately, by dissolving. The
boundary between self and world is thin, which is a gift and a tax. Itch: form —
banks that let the river actually flow somewhere.

## Houses — the settings (where the story stages itself)

Angular houses (1, 4, 7, 10) are the stages with the brightest lights — planets
there act loudly. Succedent (2, 5, 8, 11) consolidate; cadent (3, 6, 9, 12)
process and prepare.

**The horizon divides them, and the division is structural, not decorative.**
Houses 1–6 lie below it and are the self — the roots. Houses 7–12 lie above it
and are the world — the branches. Each upper house is the *output* of the lower
house facing it. Roots before branches. See `deepest-principles.md` for the six
spines and the law that governs them; the short version is that an empty house
still carries its principle at full strength, so an empty 1st is the anchor of a
crowded 7th, and the emptiness is not an absence of the task.

**1st** — The threshold: the face turned toward the world, the style of arrival,
the body's first answer to "who goes there?" Not a mask over the real self — the
front door to it.

**2nd** — The storehouse: resources, money as its symbol, worth as its substance.
What one has, and beneath that, what one believes one is worth.

**3rd** — The neighborhood: the near world of siblings, early schooling, language,
the daily mind. Where the world first got named.

**4th** — The taproot: home, family inheritance (emotional more than material),
the parent experienced as ground. The private base beneath every public act.
Conflate it with nothing — this is the *inner* home.

**5th** — The hearth-fire: creation, play, romance, children literal or
symbolic. Where one risks being seen enjoying one's own existence.

**6th** — The workshop: craft, service, routine, the body's daily maintenance.
Where devotion becomes ritual and work becomes prayer or drudgery.

**7th** — The mirror: partnership, the significant other, the open enemy. What
one meets in others is often what one has disowned — this house stages the
reunion.

**8th** — The depths: intimacy, shared resources, sexuality, death and
inheritance, the psyche's basement. Where merging with another changes what one
is. The house of transformation, not merely of crisis.

**9th** — The far horizon: philosophy, faith, higher learning, the long journey.
Where the neighborhood's answers stop sufficing.

**10th** — The summit: vocation, public standing, the parent experienced as
model or measure. Not just career — the visible answer to "what is your life
for?"

**11th** — The commons: friendship, community, the future one builds with
others. Where individual gifts join a larger current.

**12th** — The sea behind the house: the hidden, the ancestral, the collective
tide; retreat, institutions, self-undoing and self-transcendence. What lives
here works from behind the curtain until it is befriended.

## Aspects — the verbs (how the characters relate)

Aspects are relationships between drives, not grades on a report card. "Hard"
aspects generate the friction that builds engines; "soft" aspects confer gifts
that can go unexamined. Orbs: the tighter the orb, the louder the conversation
— under 2° it runs day and night.

**Conjunction (0°)** — Fusion. Two characters standing in the same spot,
sharing one costume; enormous power, little perspective. They cannot see each
other because they see *through* each other. Task: differentiation without
divorce.

**Opposition (180°)** — The negotiating table. Two characters facing off across
the axis, usually with one exiled into other people ("I keep meeting partners
who..."). Task: withdraw the projection and hold both ends — the axis is one
spine, not two enemies.

**Square (90°)** — Friction that builds engines. Two characters at cross
purposes, neither able to ignore the other. The chart's chief source of drive
and its chief complaint. Task: neither suppress one side nor alternate wildly,
but engineer — let the tension torque something into motion.

**Trine (120°)** — Native ease. Two characters who speak the same element's
language; talent that feels so natural it may never be developed. Task:
consciousness — spend the gift on purpose.

**Sextile (60°)** — The open invitation. Compatibility that responds if
approached; opportunity requiring a yes. Task: the yes.

**Quincunx (150°)** — The awkward adjustment. Two characters with no shared
language — different element, different mode — obliged to share a life anyway.
Chronic slight discomfort; task: accommodation without amputation. (Minor;
mention only when tight.)
