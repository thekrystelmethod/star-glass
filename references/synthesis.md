# The Synthesis Methodology

This file is the difference between a portrait and a pile of paragraphs. A
lookup-table reading ("Sun in X. New paragraph. Sun in the 8th. New paragraph.")
fails not because its sentences are wrong but because nobody decided what
matters most and nobody noticed that several placements are telling the same
story. Synthesis is those two acts: **weighting** and **theme detection**. Do
them *before* writing a single interpretive sentence.

The calculator (`scripts/calculate_chart.py`) pre-computes the raw material in
its `weighting` block: angularity, chart ruler, element/mode balance, stelliums,
tightest aspects, retrogrades. Your job is to turn that data into a hierarchy
and a set of themes.

## 1. Weighting — who gets to speak first

Rank the chart's voices. A useful mental model: the chart is a cast of
characters and you are casting the play. Leads, supporting roles, cameos.

**Leads (interpret always, and first):**
- The tripod: Sun, Moon, Ascendant — sign, house, and their aspects. These
  three in relation to each other are the spine of any reading.
- Angular planets (the calculator lists them): a planet within 8° of ASC, MC,
  DESC, or IC acts *publicly and loudly*, whatever else it is. A generational
  outer planet on an angle stops being generational and becomes personal.
- The chart ruler (ruler of the rising sign): its sign, house, and condition
  color the whole enterprise. Note the traditional co-ruler when the calculator
  provides one — often both speak.
- Any aspect with orb under 2° ("tight" in the output), especially to the
  tripod. A 0.1° aspect is a lifelong companion; treat it accordingly.

**Supporting roles:**
- Stelliums (3+ planets in a sign or house): a gravitational center. A stellium
  house is where the life insists on happening.
- Element/mode imbalances — and *absences above all*. A missing element is one
  of the most reliable findings in a chart: the person tends to either
  overcompensate toward it, marry it, or meet it as fate. Name the missing
  function gently and concretely.
- Squares and oppositions involving personal planets (Sun through Mars): the
  engines.
- Saturn and Chiron by house: where the fear and the wound sit — and therefore
  where the mastery and the medicine develop.

**Cameos (mention only if they join a theme):**
- Wide aspects (orb > 5°), sextiles and trines not part of a larger pattern,
  minor placements, retrogradation of outer planets (near-ubiquitous; interpret
  personal-planet retrogrades only).

When in doubt, cut. A reading that says three true things deeply beats one that
says twelve true things thinly.

## 2. Theme detection — the two-witness rule

The Greene move: scan for the same story told multiple ways. Before writing,
list candidate themes and count their witnesses. **A theme needs at least two
independent witnesses before it may lead a reading**; three or more witnesses
and it *must* lead.

Witnesses agree when different chart factors point at the same archetypal
territory. Examples of a witness set:
- Saturn–Moon square + Moon in Capricorn + Saturn in the 4th → one theme (the
  early experience of conditional warmth; feeling responsible for the emotional
  weather), three witnesses.
- Sun in the 12th + Pisces stellium + Neptune conjunct ASC → one theme (a
  porous, imaginative, boundary-thin identity), three witnesses.
- Missing air + Mercury conjunct Neptune + Moon in Pisces → one theme (knowing
  arrives as feeling and image rather than argument), three witnesses.

Mechanically: for each lead placement, ask "what else in this chart says
this?" — same planet emphasized twice (by sign, house, and aspect), same house
occupied and ruled significantly, same element repeated or absent, dispositor
chains landing on one planet. Where witnesses converge, a theme exists. Where a
single loud placement has *no* second witness, present it as a distinct
subplot, not the main story.

Contradictions are themes too — often the best ones. Mars in Aries square
Saturn in Cancer is not noise to smooth over; it is the inner drama itself.
Present contradictions in "part of you… while another part…" language: the
psyche is a parliament, and the chart is its seating plan. Never average two
contradictory placements into beige.

## 3. The narrative arc (portrait mode)

A full portrait follows a developmental arc, roughly Greene's implicit
structure. Use these movements as the skeleton; retitle them to fit the person:

1. **Overture — temperament and type.** Element/mode balance, the tripod, the
   chart ruler. The reader should recognize their own texture within two
   paragraphs.
2. **The ground floor — early environment and inheritance.** 4th/10th axis,
   Moon and Saturn, aspects to the luminaries. Frame as *the child's
   experience*, not an indictment of actual parents.
3. **The inner cast and its conflicts.** The two or three detected themes, each
   with its witnesses woven together — never listed placement-by-placement.
   This is the longest movement.
4. **The mirror — love and relation.** Venus, Mars, the 7th and 8th, the DESC.
   What is sought, what is projected, what intimacy asks this person to
   develop.
5. **The summit — vocation and calling.** MC, 10th, 6th, the Sun's aim, the
   North Node's direction. Vocation as *what the psyche is for*, beyond job
   titles.
6. **The integration challenge.** The hardest configuration — usually the
   outer-planet or Chiron material — reframed as the life's curriculum, and the
   nodal axis as its direction of travel. End with the developmental
   invitation, not a summary.

Conversational mode uses the same weighting and themes but answers only what
was asked — *in context*. "What does my Venus mean?" is answered with Venus's
sign, house, and aspects woven together with any theme Venus participates in,
in a few strong paragraphs — not with three separate lookups, and not with the
whole chart.

## 4. Restraint rules — the ethics of the register

These are not disclaimers to append; they are how the interpretation itself is
written.

- **No fatalism.** The chart describes the cast, never the script's ending.
  Every hard configuration is written as a developmental task with a mature
  expression the person can grow into — and the mature expression is described
  *concretely*, not as a consolation prize.
- **No fortune-telling, no event prediction, no medical, mortality, financial,
  or legal forecasts.** Not "your marriage will struggle" but "intimacy tends
  to ask this of you."
- **Tendencies, not sentences.** "Tends to," "may find," "often experiences" —
  the language of pattern, not decree. But do not hedge into mush: name the
  pattern vividly, then leave the person free.
- **Dignity.** Write about the shadow the way a good analyst speaks of a
  defense: as something that once protected, now costs. Never sneer at a
  placement. There are no bad charts.
- **The parents are people.** 4th/10th and Moon/Saturn material describes the
  child's *experience* of caregiving, which can differ from the caregivers'
  intent or character. Write it that way.
- **Astrology's standing.** The reading is offered as a symbolic, reflective
  language — a mirror for self-inquiry in the psychological-astrology
  tradition, not an empirical claim about causation. Carry this in tone (no
  cosmic determinism) rather than in repeated disclaimers; one light touch at
  the start of a portrait suffices.
